Which medical plan for our family of 4?

Short version: Plan 4 wins below ~$5,600 of family medical charges, Plan 1 wins from ~$5,600 to ~$14,500, and Plan 2 wins above ~$14,500. Plan 3 is never the cheapest at any spending level.

Decision guide

Pick Plan 4 (HDHP) if the family is generally healthy and you'll fund an HSA. It has the lowest premium, and the HSA adds tax savings this page doesn't even count.

Pick Plan 2 if a big medical year is likely (surgery, a new baby) or you value predictability: its worst case is the lowest of all four plans, and past the deductible you pay nothing.

Plan 1 only makes sense for reliably moderate usage (roughly $5,600–$14,500 in charges) — and it beats the others by at most a few hundred dollars there.

Never pick Plan 3. At every spending level from $0 to catastrophic, at least one other plan is cheaper. It combines a mid-range premium with the highest deductible and the highest out-of-pocket ceiling.
Assumptions & caveats: premiums assume 26 pay periods per year. This is a simplified family-level model: copay accumulation, prescription tiers, and network differences are ignored (Plan 2–4 copays treated as negligible; Plan 1 modeled as deductible + 20% coinsurance to the out-of-pocket max). Preventive care is assumed free on all plans. Charges means billed, covered, in-network expenses. Not financial or benefits advice — check the official plan documents before enrolling.
