Your medical plan, in one idea

You pay the full discounted price for most care until you reach the yearly cap. After that, the plan pays 100% of covered in-network care for the rest of the year.

The HSA that comes with this plan

A Health Savings Account is a tax-free wallet for medical costs. It is the piece that makes a high-deductible plan work.

  • Money goes in tax-free, grows tax-free, and pays for medical costs tax-free.
  • Use it for the spending in step 2 — visits, prescriptions, labs.
  • Whatever you don't use rolls over every year and stays yours, even if you change jobs.
Two things to watch: stay in-network (out-of-network costs far more), and if a hospital or specialist wants to do something out-of-network, get it pre-approved or there is a $250 penalty.

Your dental plan

The plan pays up to $1,000 per person per year. There's a $100 deductible per person ($300 for the family) — but it does not apply to cleanings and check-ups, which are always free.

100%
Cleanings, exams, X-raysTwice-yearly cleanings, fluoride for kids. No deductible.
70%
Fillings, extractions, root canals, gum carePlan pays 70%, you pay 30%, after the $100.
50%
Crowns, dentures, bridges, implantsPlan pays 50%, you pay 50%, after the $100.
Use an in-network (contracted) dentist to pay the least. The $1,000 yearly limit covers routine care well; big work like a crown can use up a lot of it, so spread major work across calendar years when you can.

Decision guide

Pick Plan 4 (HDHP) if the family is generally healthy and you'll fund an HSA. It has the lowest premium, and the HSA adds tax savings this page doesn't even count.

Pick Plan 2 if a big medical year is likely (surgery, a new baby) or you value predictability: its worst case is the lowest of all four plans, and past the deductible you pay nothing.

Plan 1 only makes sense for reliably moderate usage (roughly $5,600–$14,500 in charges) — and it beats the others by at most a few hundred dollars there.

Never pick Plan 3. At every spending level from $0 to catastrophic, at least one other plan is cheaper. It combines a mid-range premium with the highest deductible and the highest out-of-pocket ceiling.
